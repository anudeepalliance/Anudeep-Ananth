You are quality-checking a scored account list before it is used for a B2B cross-sell campaign at Ocrolus, a
document-automation platform for lenders.

Each row is one customer account with its raw data, three CSM-note scores (0-10 buying signal per add-on product,
hyphen = already adopted), a risk flag, and three computed priority scores (0-100, one per add-on product).

Your job: for each account, judge how likely it is that the row contains an ANOMALY, meaning something that would make
the computed priority misleading if a person did not look at it first. Give an anomaly score from 0 to 10.

What counts as an anomaly:

- Numbers out of line with peers: for example ARR far too high or low for the processing volume compared with similar
  accounts.
- Fields that contradict each other: for example a positive note with a very low NPS, or a note that says "fully
  adopted" while an adoption flag says N.
- Mixed signals: a strong buying signal together with weak health (low NPS, long gap since last QBR, renewal very
  close).
- No clear winner: two or three products tie on priority with no product named in the note.
- Anything else in the row that a careful account manager would want to check before outreach.

What does NOT count as an anomaly:

- An account that is simply low priority, small, or neutral.
- An account that is correctly excluded (flagged as risk, priority 0) with data that is consistent.
- A short or unremarkable note.

Scoring scale:
0-1  = nothing unusual; row is internally consistent
2-3  = minor oddity, no effect on the ranking
4    = worth a glance, but the ranking is probably still right
5-7  = likely anomaly; a person should review before outreach
8-10 = clear anomaly or contradiction; do not act on the computed priority without review

Rules:

1. Compare each account against the other accounts in the list, not against outside knowledge.
2. Judge only from the fields provided. Do not infer anything from the company name.
3. For every score of 5 or higher, anomaly_reason must state the specific fields and values that caused it, in one short
   sentence.
4. For scores of 4 or lower, write a hyphen (-) as the anomaly_reason.

Return exactly two values per account: anomaly_score (a whole number from 0 to 10) and anomaly_reason.

ACCOUNTS (one JSON object per line):
{{ACCOUNT_ROWS}}

OUTPUT FORMAT
Return ONLY a JSON object, with no text before or after it and no code fences. Include every account exactly once.
{"accounts":[{"accountId":"A001","anomalyScore":1,"anomalyReason":"-"}]}
